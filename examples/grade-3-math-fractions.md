# Grade 3 math: unit fractions

> **Teacher request.** Write a 45-minute Grade 3 lesson introducing unit fractions, with a worksheet and an exit ticket. Standard: CCSS 3.NF.A.1. 24 students, two are newcomer English learners. We have fraction strips and mini whiteboards.

## Objectives (`edu-learning-objectives`)

| Objective | Level | Success criteria | Standard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explain that a unit fraction 1/b is one part when a whole is split into b equal parts. | DOK 2 | I can tell if parts are equal. I can say what the bottom number means. I can name one part as 1/b. | CCSS 3.NF.A.1 |
| Draw and label a unit fraction of a shape or strip. | DOK 1 | I can split a shape into equal parts. I can shade and label one part. | CCSS 3.NF.A.1 |

Priority if time runs short: the first objective.

## Lesson plan (`edu-lesson-planner`)

**Objective (student language).** I can explain what 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, and 1/8 mean, and I can show one of them on a shape.

**Warm-up (5 min).** Show two drawings of a sandwich cut into two pieces: one cut down the middle, one cut near the edge. Ask: "Two friends share this sandwich. Which way is fair? Why?" Students turn and talk, then three share. Listen for "same size" and write "equal parts" on the board.

**I do (10 min).** Hold up a whole fraction strip. Fold a paper strip into 4 equal parts.

Think-aloud: "I folded my strip into 4 parts. Are they equal? I check by lining them up. Yes. One part is one out of four equal parts. We write that as 1/4. The 4 on the bottom tells how many equal parts make the whole. The 1 on top tells we are talking about one part."

Repeat with a strip folded into 2, and show that 1/2 is bigger than 1/4. Say: "More equal parts means each part is smaller."

**We do (12 min).** Students use fraction strips and whiteboards.

1. "Find the strip with 3 equal parts. Hold up one part. Write its fraction." (1/3)
2. "Find 1/8. How many of these make one whole?" (8)
3. Show a circle cut into 4 unequal parts with one shaded. "Is the shaded part 1/4?" Expected wrong answer: "Yes, because there are 4 parts." Push: "Are the parts equal?" (No, so it is not 1/4.)

**You do (15 min).** The worksheet below. Early finishers do the challenge item.

**Checks for understanding.**

- During "we do": whiteboards held up for items 1 and 2. If more than a few boards are wrong, repeat with the 1/2 strip.
- Hinge question after "we do": "Which picture shows 1/3?" with A: a rectangle cut into 3 equal parts, one shaded (correct). B: a rectangle cut into 3 unequal parts, one shaded (ignores equal parts). C: a rectangle cut into 4 equal parts, one shaded (counts shaded vs. unshaded: 1 shaded, 3 not). Students show fingers 1, 2, or 3.
- Exit ticket at the end (below).

**Closure (3 min).** Students finish the sentence on a whiteboard: "The bottom number in 1/6 tells me ___." Read the success criteria aloud and students show a thumb up, sideways, or down for each.

**Differentiation.**

- Support: the two newcomer students get a word bank with pictures (whole, equal parts, one part, fraction) and the frame "1/__ means 1 of __ equal parts." Pair each with a supportive partner for "we do".
- Extension: "Is 1/3 or 1/5 of the same pizza bigger? Draw to prove it."

**Materials.** Fraction strips (one set per pair), mini whiteboards and markers, paper strips for folding, copies of the worksheet and exit ticket, sandwich drawings for the warm-up.

## Worksheet (`edu-worksheet-builder`)

**Unit fractions**

Name: ________ Date: ________

I can explain what a fraction like 1/4 means, and show it on a shape.

**Example.** This rectangle has 4 equal parts. One part is shaded. The shaded part is **1/4**.

1. **Circle** the shapes that are cut into equal parts. (Picture: a square in 2 equal halves, a circle in 3 unequal parts, a rectangle in 4 equal parts, a triangle in 2 unequal parts.)
2. A rectangle has 3 equal parts. One part is shaded. **Write** the fraction for the shaded part. ________
3. A circle has 8 equal parts. One part is shaded. **Write** the fraction. ________
4. **Draw** a line to split this square into 2 equal parts. Shade 1/2.
5. **Split** this strip into 6 equal parts. Shade 1/6.
6. How many 1/4 parts make one whole? ________
7. **Fill in.** 1/3 means 1 of ____ equal parts.
8. Sam says this shape shows 1/4 because it has 4 parts. (Picture: a rectangle cut into 4 unequal parts, one shaded.) Is Sam right? **Explain** why or why not.

**Challenge.** Which is bigger, 1/2 or 1/8 of the same strip? Draw to show how you know.

## Exit ticket (`edu-formative-checks`)

1. A pizza is cut into 6 equal slices. You eat 1 slice. What fraction did you eat? ________
2. Draw a rectangle. Show 1/3 of it.

| Answer | What it shows | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/6, and a rectangle in 3 equal parts with one shaded | Meets both objectives | Move on to fractions a/b tomorrow |
| 1/5 or 5/6 on item 1 | Counts the slices not eaten, or names the leftover part | Small group: name the shaded part with fraction strips |
| 6/1 on item 1 | Reverses numerator and denominator | Reteach "the bottom number is how many equal parts" with folding |
| Rectangle in 3 unequal parts | Doesn't yet see equal parts as required | Repeat the sandwich warm-up with the student; fold strips |

Sort rule: if fewer than 20 of 24 students get both items, open tomorrow with a 10-minute reteach before moving on.
