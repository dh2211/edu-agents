# Grade 8 science: chemical reactions

> **Teacher request.** Plan a 6-lesson Grade 8 unit on chemical reactions, 50-minute periods. Standard: NGSS MS-PS1-2. Then write a 10-question quiz with an answer key for after lesson 3, and a rubric for the lab report. We have a lab room with goggles. Mixed-ability class of 30.

## Unit plan (`edu-unit-planner`)

### Stage 1: desired results

**Enduring understandings**

- In a chemical reaction, atoms rearrange to make new substances with different properties from the starting substances.
- We decide whether a reaction happened by comparing properties before and after, not by appearance alone.

**Essential questions**

- How do we know when something new has formed?
- Is every change you can see a chemical change?

**Objectives**

| Objective | DOK | Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify characteristic properties of a substance (density, melting point, solubility, flammability, odor). | 1 | NGSS MS-PS1-2 |
| Analyze before-and-after property data to decide whether a chemical reaction occurred. | 3 | NGSS MS-PS1-2 |
| Distinguish evidence of a chemical change from a physical change and explain the difference. | 2 | NGSS MS-PS1-2 |
| Write a lab report that makes a claim supported by evidence and reasoning. | 3 | [standard: ask teacher] |

**Likely misconceptions**

- Any bubbles mean a chemical reaction (boiling water also bubbles).
- Dissolving makes a substance disappear or makes a new substance.
- A color change always means a new substance (food coloring mixing is physical).

### Stage 2: assessment evidence

**Performance task (GRASPS).** You are a lab technician (role) for a food company. The company wants to know whether mixing its new powder with water makes a new substance (situation). Test the mixture, compare properties before and after, and write a lab report for the quality manager (audience, product). Success means a claim backed by property data and reasoning (standards).

**Other evidence**

| When | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Lesson 1 | Pre-assessment: 5 sort cards (physical or chemical change) |
| Lessons 1 to 6 | Exit ticket each lesson |
| After lesson 3 | 10-question quiz (below) |
| Lessons 5 and 6 | Performance task lab report, scored with the rubric (below) |

### Stage 3: learning plan

| Lesson | Objective | Key activity | Formative check | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Identify properties | Pre-assessment sort; property stations (measure density of two liquids, test solubility of salt and sand) | Exit ticket: name 3 properties of a substance in the room | Balances, graduated cylinders, salt, sand, water |
| 2 | Distinguish physical and chemical changes | Demonstrations: melt ice, dissolve sugar, burn a wooden splint (teacher only) | Hinge question: is dissolving sugar chemical? | Hot plate, sugar, splints, fire extinguisher nearby |
| 3 | Analyze property data | Data cards: before and after tables for 4 mystery changes; groups decide and justify | Quiz (after lesson) | Printed data cards |
| 4 | Analyze property data | Lab: baking soda and vinegar; measure temperature change, observe gas | Exit ticket: claim and two pieces of evidence | Goggles, baking soda, vinegar, thermometers, cups |
| 5 | Lab report writing | Performance task lab: powder and water; collect data | Teacher checks data tables during lab | Teacher-chosen safe powder [verify with the school's chemical safety list], goggles |
| 6 | Lab report writing | Write and peer review report with the rubric student checklist | Peer review checklist | Rubric copies |

Differentiation matters most in lessons 3 and 6. Use the `edu-differentiation` skill for data cards with sentence frames and a claim-evidence-reasoning organizer.

**Safety.** Goggles for all of lessons 2, 4, and 5. Only the teacher handles flame. Check every chemical against the school's lab safety rules before lesson 5.

## Quiz after lesson 3 (`edu-assessment-builder`)

### Blueprint

| Objective | Items | DOK | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identify properties | 1, 2, 3 | 1 | 3 |
| Distinguish physical and chemical change | 4, 5, 6, 7 | 2 | 4 |
| Analyze property data | 8, 9, 10 | 2, 3 | 7 |

11 of 14 points are at DOK 2 or higher.

### Student copy

**Chemical reactions quiz.** 25 minutes. Answer every question.

1. Which is a characteristic property that can help identify a substance? (1 point)
   A. Mass B. Density C. Volume D. Shape
2. A student measures the temperature at which a solid turns to liquid. Which property is this? (1 point)
   A. Boiling point B. Solubility C. Melting point D. Density
3. Which property describes how well a substance dissolves in water? (1 point)
   A. Solubility B. Flammability C. Density D. Odor
4. Which change makes a new substance? (1 point)
   A. Ice melting B. Sugar dissolving in tea C. Paper burning D. Glass breaking
5. Water boils and forms bubbles. Why is this **NOT** a chemical change? (1 point)
   A. The bubbles are water in gas form, still the same substance.
   B. Boiling only happens at high temperatures.
   C. No color change happened.
   D. The water was heated by a stove.
6. Which observation is the best evidence that a chemical reaction happened? (1 point)
   A. A liquid takes the shape of its container.
   B. Two clear liquids are mixed and a solid forms.
   C. A metal bar is bent.
   D. Salt disappears when stirred into water.
7. Food coloring is added to water and the water turns blue. A student says a new substance formed. Is the student right? (1 point)
   A. Yes, because the color changed.
   B. Yes, because the water looks different.
   C. No, because the coloring and water are only mixed.
   D. No, because blue is not a new color.
8. Use the table. (2 points)

   | Property | Substance X (before) | Substance Y (after heating X) |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Color | White | Black |
   | Melting point | 186 °C | Does not melt, burns |
   | Solubility in water | Dissolves | Does not dissolve |

   Did a chemical reaction happen? Name two properties that are evidence.
9. Salt and water are mixed. The table shows what the teacher measured. (2 points)

   | Property | Before | After |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Appearance | Clear liquid and white crystals | One clear liquid |
   | Density | 1.00 g/cm³ (water) and 2.16 g/cm³ (salt) | 1.07 g/cm³ (liquid) |
   | After the water evaporates | | White crystals with density 2.16 g/cm³ remain |

   A student says a new substance formed because there is only one liquid now. Explain whether the data supports the student.
10. Design a test. You think mixing two liquids makes a new substance. Describe two properties you would measure before and after, and what results would show a reaction. (3 points)

### Answer key

| Item | Answer | DOK | Points | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | B | 1 | 1 | Density does not depend on amount. Mass (A) and volume (C) do, which is a common confusion. Shape (D) is not a property of the substance. |
| 2 | C | 1 | 1 | A confuses melting and boiling. B and D are other properties. |
| 3 | A | 1 | 1 | B, C, D are properties, but not about dissolving. |
| 4 | C | 2 | 1 | Burning makes ash, smoke, and gases. A, B, D are physical changes; B targets the dissolving misconception. |
| 5 | A | 2 | 1 | B and D describe the conditions, not the substance. C targets "no color change means no reaction". |
| 6 | B | 2 | 1 | A new solid (precipitate) is evidence of a new substance. D targets the dissolving misconception. |
| 7 | C | 2 | 1 | A and B target "color change always means reaction". D is irrelevant. |
| 8 | Yes. Any two of: color changed, melting point changed, solubility changed. | 2 | 2 | 1 point for "yes", 1 point for two correct properties. Substance X's data matches sugar [verify if you name it to students]. |
| 9 | No. The salt dissolved. When the water evaporates, crystals with the same density as salt (2.16 g/cm³) come back, so no new substance formed. One liquid alone is not evidence of a new substance. | 3 | 2 | 1 point for "not supported", 1 point for reasoning that uses the recovered crystals or density. Targets the dissolving misconception. |
| 10 | Any two characteristic properties (density, melting or boiling point, solubility, color with another property, odor). A reaction is shown when the after values differ from both starting substances. | 3 | 3 | 1 point per property (2), 1 point for stating that results must differ from both starting substances. |

## Lab report rubric (`edu-rubric-builder`)

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Claim | States whether a new substance formed and limits the claim to what the data shows. | States whether a new substance formed. | States a claim that is unclear or only partly answers the question. | States no claim, or a claim unrelated to the question. |
| Evidence | Uses at least two property measurements from before and after, with units. | Uses two property observations from before and after; units missing or one is qualitative. | Uses one before-and-after observation. | Uses no data, or data from another group. |
| Reasoning | Explains how each piece of evidence supports the claim, using the idea that new substances have different properties. | Explains how the evidence supports the claim, without naming the property idea. | Repeats the evidence without explaining why it matters. | Gives no reasoning. |
| Data table | Table has labeled columns, units, and before and after values for every property tested. | Table has labels and values; one unit or value missing. | Table is incomplete; several values or labels missing. | No table. |
| Safety and procedure | Lists safety steps used and procedure steps another student could repeat. | Lists safety steps and most procedure steps. | Lists safety or procedure, not both. | Lists neither. |

**Student checklist**

- I said whether a new substance formed.
- I used two before-and-after measurements, with units.
- I explained why my evidence shows a new substance or not.
- My table has labels, units, and all my data.
- I wrote my safety steps and a procedure someone could repeat.

Total: 20 points. The teacher's grading scale was not given, so no conversion is included.
